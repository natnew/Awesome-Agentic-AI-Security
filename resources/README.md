# Resources

This section holds curated external resources for agentic AI security. It is not intended to become a flat list of links. Each substantial entry should explain why the resource matters, what it covers, how mature it is, and where its limitations are.

The resource catalogue should help readers distinguish standards, frameworks, vendor research, independent research, tools, benchmarks, guides, and opinion pieces.

## Available Now

- [Standards And Frameworks](standards-and-frameworks.md) collects community frameworks, standards, and public guidance for AI and agentic security.
- [Vendor Research](vendor-research.md) labels vendor-produced research, architecture guidance, and field observations with caveats.
- [Papers](papers.md) collects academic, independent, and research-oriented work on agentic attacks, memory, prompt injection, evaluation, and system security.
- [Tools](tools.md) lists defensive tools for red teaming, evaluation, observability, inventory, and runtime control.
- [Benchmarks](benchmarks.md) collects benchmarks, testbeds, and evaluation methods relevant to agentic behaviour.
- [Cyber-Capable AI Agents And Autonomous Vulnerability Discovery](cyber-capable-ai-agents.md) tracks Mythos / Glasswing, CyberGym, ExploitGym, disclosure, patch verification, sanitizers, open-weight risk, and the convergence of AI engineering with security engineering.

## Coverage Areas

The resource catalogue covers:

- Standards and frameworks.
- Vendor research.
- Independent research and papers.
- Tools for securing, observing, testing, or governing agentic systems.
- Benchmarks and evaluation harnesses.
- Cyber-capable AI agents, autonomous vulnerability discovery, disclosure workflows, patch verification, and frontier cyber governance.

## Entry Quality

Substantial resource entries should include:

- Resource type.
- Producer or publisher.
- Source link.
- Relevance to agentic execution security.
- Risks, behaviours, controls, benchmarks, or architectures covered.
- Evidence quality and maturity level.
- Last-checked date.
- Important limitations or caveats.

## How To Read Entries

The catalogue is evidence for security judgement, not a list of endorsements. Standards and frameworks help organise risk, vendor research can surface useful field observations, papers can test emerging ideas, tools can support evaluation workflows, and benchmarks can provide bounded signals about behaviour. None of these categories proves that a deployed agentic system is secure by itself.

When comparing entries, pay close attention to the maturity and limitations fields. A resource may be useful for threat modelling, control design, assurance evidence, or research direction while still being narrow, early, vendor-specific, or incomplete.

## Selection Principle

The repository favours fewer, better-labelled resources over a large unstructured catalogue. A resource should strengthen the reader's understanding of agentic execution security, practical control design, evaluation evidence, governance, or research direction.
